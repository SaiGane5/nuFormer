Fine-tuning Qwen3-1.7B as a nuFormer recommender

Synthetic Brazilian-bank data: 20,000 customers, 4.25 M transactions. Task: will the customer activate the offered product in months 10–12? Seven models, same 5,069 test rows.

Bottom line: the full nuFormer pipeline trains and runs end to end, but it did not beat LightGBM at this scale. Joint fusion scored 0.736 AUC vs LightGBM's 0.765 (−3.9%). It is undertrained rather than flat: validation AUC was still rising at the final step, and the transformer added a little on top of its own tabular branch (0.736 vs 0.733).

Training

  • LoRA r=16, α=32 on all 7 projections × 28 layers: 17.9 M trainable (18.3 M with DCNv2). Peak GPU memory 5.5 GB.
  • 1 epoch, batch 4 × accumulation 8 → 414 optimizer steps, about 117 min per worker in parallel; scoring took another ~67 min.
  • Validation AUC, joint: 0.629 → 0.705 → 0.712 → 0.718. Sequence only: 0.557 → 0.589 → 0.619 → 0.623.
  • Tokenizer: 13.7 tokens per transaction, so 512 tokens hold the last ~37 transactions of a median 205.

Why it fell short of the paper

  • Too little training. 13,265 training rows and one epoch. nuFormer's +1.25% came from millions of customers. The rising validation curve says more steps would help.
  • Context too short. About 6 weeks of history are visible. The simulator's life events (raise, new baby, travel plans) often happen months earlier, while LightGBM's 90-day aggregates still see them.
  • Weak product conditioning. Sequence-only ranks the 5 products worse than chance (Spearman −0.22): a one-hot offer added after pooling can't separate the products with ~1,700 positives.

Next run, in order

  1. Context 2,048 tokens (fits a T4 with gradient checkpointing), or shorter descriptions to cut tokens per transaction.
  2. A multi-task head with one logit per product, trained on all 5 labels per customer instead of one offer per row.
  3. 3 epochs and 50k+ customers; the generator scales linearly.
